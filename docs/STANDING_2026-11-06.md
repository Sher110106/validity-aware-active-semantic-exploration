# Where we stand against the ASP paper (6 Nov 2026)

**In short:**
- We now score each stage of our runs with the author's own F1 and GED code, on his time axis.
- On that footing the paper's result splits by scene in our runs:
  - On 853, frontier is at or above ASP, as in our earlier tests.
  - On 871, ASP leads frontier at matched times, as in the paper. But his code clocks the frontier explorer in a way that may account for part of that lead.
- We cannot copy his reference graph or his time budget without him, so those are what to ask for.

## 1. What now matches the paper
- **Pipeline:** his released code, commit f1ea141b, with our opt-in changes: DeepSeek in place of Gemini, the filter and random-guess arms, and a small random shift of the frontier start.
- **F1:** his rule from `f1_score_plot.py`.
  - A match needs the same name and centres closer than 0.5 m.
  - Objects are matched greedily in file order, and doors are dropped.
  - The score is the mean over the two tracked graphs.

  An independent recompute matched every number to within 0.0005.
- **GED:** his own `ged_score_plot.py` code (gmatch4py with his costs), now running on the host. It gives an approximate GED, as his did.
- **Object list:** our 853 and 871 runs load his default 52-label file. His code also has a 61-label file made for scene 573 (the 52 plus 9). That fits the paper's "61 categories", but the paper does not say which scene used which.
- **Time axis:** the navigation time each stage logs, the field his scripts read. It is wall time while the robot follows a path. It leaves out the 360° scan at each stop, planning, and LLM calls.

## 2. What still differs
| | Paper | Ours |

| Reference graph | a tour driven by hand through the same pipeline; never released | the HM3D human labels (180 objects on 853, 232 on 871), and one frontier run's final map (80 and 91 objects). A scripted tour that follows his protocol is built and waiting for the GPU. |
| Time budget | 10%, 50% and 100% of a budget whose value is not given | for now, the end of one frontier run: 632 s on 853 and 809 s on 871. Once the new frontier runs finish, the budget becomes their median end, and every number in §4 will move. |
| Run length | L_max not given; 25-30 steps of about 3.74 m suggest 94-112 m | ASP runs stop at 60 m, about 380 s. All of them have 10% values. Every 853 run reaches 50%, but only 2 of 12 on 871 do, and none reaches 100%. |

| Runs per scene | apparently one. His 853 numbers at 100% moved between v1 and v2 (frontier 48.3 to 58.8, ASP 59.0 to 66.7). | 2-3 per arm |
| Computer | a laptop with an RTX 4060 | a shared server; the clock is wall time, so speed can differ |

## 3. Are our graphs like his?
Probably in kind and roughly in size, but this is not proven.
- **His reference behaves like a mapper-made graph.** His GED falls as his runs go on (853 frontier: 279 to 177). Ours does the same against our frontier map. Against the human labels, ours rises (552 to 836), because the labels hold many objects the detector never finds.
- **Size.** An empty graph scores 285 and 318 against our frontier map (gmatch4py counts nodes plus twice the edges), and 552 and 712 against the human labels. His frontier's 10% values, 279 and 292, sit near the first pair. A 10% map is not empty, so this is only a rough guide.
- **What settles it.** Only his files can. Our tours give a closer copy, not proof.

What the table says:
- **The paper's margin.** At 50%, the paper's ASP leads frontier by 14.3 points on 853 and 6.7 on 871.
- **853 at 50%.** Our frontier's mean is above both ASP arms: by 2.5-15.2 points on FM, and by 2.6-5.2 on HL, where the ranges overlap.
  - Frontier leads even though it had walked less by then (39-42 m, against 45-49 m for ASP).
  - Random guesses land between the two ASP arms.
  - Frontier stays on top at a 20% shorter and a 20% longer budget.
- **871.** At 50% of the budget most ASP runs had already stopped. At 50% of a 20% shorter budget all three ASP runs reach it, and they lead frontier by 14.2 points (FM) and 8.0 (HL). They also lead random guesses.
  - By then ASP had walked farther (46-55 m against 33-37 m), probably in part because frontier's clock includes its path waits.
  - So part of this lead may be the clock, not better exploration.
- **10%.**
  - On 853 his ASP starts 15.8 points above his frontier. Ours starts level with or just below our frontier.
  - On 871 our ASP is close to his (33.3-34.2 against 30.5), and our frontier is higher than his (37.6 against 29.2).
- **Spread.**
  - On 853 at 50%, our two DeepSeek arms, which differ only in the filter, sit 12.7 points apart on FM, with no overlap. On HL they sit 2.5 apart.
  - Our pre-registered test of these arms (E2: area under the curve to 60 m, on HL) found no detectable difference.
  - With 2-3 runs per arm we cannot tell spread from a real effect at one time point.

## 5. So where do we stand?
- **Method parity: nearly done.** Each stage is scored with his F1 and GED code. Two things are still open: which label file each scene used, and how Fig. 4 reads a value at each time.
- **Graph parity: likely, not proven.**
- **Result parity: split, and descriptive only (2-3 runs).**
  - On 853 we do not get his ASP lead. This fits our pre-registered frontier test from 10-30, which found no detectable difference by path length against human labels.
  - On 871 his direction appears at matched times, but his clock charges frontier for path waits that ASP never pays.
- **The fair statement.** We cannot say his numbers are wrong. We can say his 853 result does not hold in our runs, and that his released code times the two explorers differently, in ASP's favour, in a way the paper does not mention.

## 7. What is running
- **The host queue is waiting.**
  - Another user's vLLM server holds about 46 of the GPU's 48 GB. Our first run hit "CUDA out of memory" at 17:01 and was stopped.
  - The queue checks every 5 minutes and starts a job only when 12 GB is free on two checks in a row. It also needs 25 GB of host memory and 10 GB of free disk.
- **The queue's 15 jobs, in order:**
  1. a 3-waypoint tour dry run; if it fails, the tours are cancelled;
  2. frontier to its natural end on 853 and 871;
  3. four scripted tours, two per scene;
  4. eight more frontier seeds. Seeds 45-46 run only if disk allows.

  Each job is capped at 4 hours, and we expect most to take about an hour. All are $0.
- **When it finishes:**
  - The tours give a reference built as close to his way as a script can, if the two tours of a scene agree (pre-registered check: F1 of at least 0.90).
  - The frontier runs set the budget to their median end and fill frontier's 100% column. ASP runs stop at 60 m, so they still have no 100% value.
